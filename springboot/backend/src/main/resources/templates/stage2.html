🌿 Tillering Stage of Sugarcane

✕

Duration: ~90 to 120 days after planting, Approx duration 2.5 month i.e. 75 days

The plant begins producing leaves actively after ~30-35 days.

Processes:

Multiple shoots (called tillers) emerge from the base of the original shoot.
This stage determines the final shoot population, which directly influences yield.

Nutrient Requirement:
Nitrogen is most important during this stage.
Adequate nitrogen ensures vigorous tiller growth and healthy leaf development.

Significance:
The number of tillers that survive indicates how much sugarcane will grow later.
A strong tillering stage sets the foundation for the Grand Growth Stage.

🌾 IMPORTANT FACTORS FOR TILLERING

To promote maximum tillering in sugarcane, focus on healthy buds, adequate sunlight and optimum temperature, and proper water management.

🌱 Healthy Buds & Planting

✔ Use healthy, disease-free setts
✔ Plant setts 5–10 cm deep
✔ Spacing: 20–30 cm setts, 90 cm rows
✔ Apply Nitrogen in early growth
✔ Avoid extreme temperatures

☀️ Sunlight & Temperature

✔ Requires full sunlight
✔ Proper row orientation
✔ Ideal temperature: 20–30°C
✔ Avoid below 13°C / above 32°C
✔ Warm humid climate preferred

💧 Water Management

✔ Maintain 60–70% soil moisture
✔ 8–10 day interval later
✔ Ensure proper drainage
✔ Use Drip irrigation for efficiency

🦠 SUGARCANE DISEASE MANAGEMENT

Sugarcane is affected by fungal, bacterial, viral, and phytoplasma diseases, causing yield and sugar loss. Integrated Disease Management is the most effective solution.

🔴 Fungal Diseases

Red Rot (Colletotrichum falcatum)
Red internal tissues, sour smell, hollow canes, heavy yield loss

Smut (Ustilago scitaminea)
Black whip-like structures, spread by wind & infected setts

Wilt (Fusarium/Cephalosporium)
Yellowing, stunting, hollow stalks

🦠 Bacterial Disease

Ratoon Stunting (Leifsonia xyli)
Stunted growth, thin stalks, fewer tillers

🧬 Viral Diseases

Mosaic Virus
Yellow-green mottling on leaves

Yellow Leaf Disease
Reduced photosynthesis and vigor

🌿 Phytoplasma Disease

Grassy Shoot
Excess tillering, narrow chlorotic leaves, bushy clumps

🌱 FYM / Compost

Rich source of organic matter & micronutrients
Improves soil structure, aeration & water-holding capacity
Enhances beneficial soil microorganisms
Reduces Red Rot, Wilt & root diseases
Application: 10–25 tons/ha

🪱 Vermicompost

Contains beneficial microbes & enzymes
Enhances root growth & nutrient availability
Suppresses soilborne pathogens
Improves cane vigor & stress resistance
Application: 2–5 tons/ha

🦠 Trichoderma harzianum

Powerful fungal biocontrol agent
Controls Red Rot, Wilt & Pokkah Boeng
Destroys pathogens by mycoparasitism
Competes for nutrients & space
Sett treatment: 10 g/kg setts
Soil application: 10 kg/ha

🌿 Azospirillum & PSB

Azospirillum fixes atmospheric nitrogen
PSB solubilizes phosphorus
Improves root development & nutrient uptake
Enhances plant vigor & disease tolerance
Application: Sett dip or soil application

🌰 Neem Cake

Natural antifungal & antibacterial properties
Controls soilborne fungi & nematodes
Reduces early-stage insect pests
Improves soil microbial balance
Application: 500–1000 kg/ha

🧪 Boron & Manganese

Strengthens cell walls
Activates enzymes
Improves sugar transport
Reduces Red Rot & Wilt susceptibility
Sett treatment: 40 ppm for 10 minutes

Select seed cane from disease-free certified fields
Use setts with healthy buds and proper moisture
Avoid discolored or fungus-infected setts
Do not use setts from infected or ratooned fields

Eliminates fungal, bacterial and phytoplasma pathogens
Hot Water Treatment: 50–52°C for 30–120 minutes
Chemical treatment using Carbendazim or Mancozeb (if needed)
Prevents Red Rot, Smut, Wilt and Grassy Shoot infections

🌾 3. Resistant Variety Planting

Resistant varieties reduce disease severity
Limits disease spread within the field
Better adaptation to climate and stress conditions
Ensures stable yield and sugar recovery
Resistance is the most economical disease control method

🌿 4. Organic Manure + Trichoderma

FYM, compost and vermicompost improve soil health
Enhances beneficial microbial population
Trichoderma suppresses soil-borne fungal pathogens
Protects roots and basal stem region
Reduces dependence on chemical fungicides

👁️ 5. Regular Monitoring

Inspect fields regularly during crop growth
Focus on early vegetative and tillering stages
Look for leaf discoloration and whip smut symptoms
Identify stunted or bushy plants early
Monitor aphids and leafhoppers for disease spread

🧹 6. Removal of Infected Plants

Immediately uproot and destroy infected clumps
Prevents secondary spread of pathogens
Protects nearby healthy plants
Avoid composting diseased plants
Maintain strict field sanitation

⚠️ 7. Chemical Control (If Required)

Use fungicides for fungal diseases
Use insecticides for vector control
Follow recommended dosage and timing
Avoid overuse to prevent resistance and environmental damage

Nitrogen N

Nitrogen is necessary for canes growth

Nitrogen is essential for sugarcane growth and development. It promotes vigorous vegetative growth, increases tiller formation, and helps in the production of healthy green leaves, which are necessary for photosynthesis.

Adequate nitrogen improves cane length, thickness, and overall yield. However, excess nitrogen can delay maturity, reduce sugar content, and make the crop more prone to pests and diseases.

For best results, nitrogen should be applied in split doses—during planting and early growth stages—and balanced with phosphorus and potassium for optimal sugar recovery.

Phosphorus P

Phosphorus is necessary for growth of roots of sugarcane & for cell division of sugarcane

Phosphorus is essential for strong root development in sugarcane, helping the plant establish quickly and absorb nutrients efficiently from the soil.

It plays a key role in cell division and energy transfer, which supports early growth, tillering, and overall plant vigor.

Adequate phosphorus improves cane formation, enhances resistance to stress, and contributes to better crop maturity and yield.

Potassium K

Potassium is necessary for increasing sucrose content of sugarcane, improves thickness of cane also leaf became healthy because of it

Potassium is essential for increasing the sucrose (sugar) content of sugarcane, thereby improving juice quality and sugar recovery.

It helps in improving cane thickness and strength, resulting in heavier and healthier canes.

Potassium also improves leaf health by enhancing photosynthesis and water regulation, making the crop more resistant to drought, pests, and diseases.

🌾 Sugarcane Balbharani Guide

What is Balbharani?

Balbharani is an important field operation carried out during the tillering stage of sugarcane. It involves application of fertilizers followed by earthing-up to strengthen the crop.

Importance

  • Encourages more tillers
  • Improves root development
  • Reduces lodging
  • Enhances nutrient uptake

Region & Timing (Kolhapur)

  • Crop Stage: 45–60 days after planting
  • Season: December–February (Adsali & Suru)
  • Soil: Black cotton & medium soils
  • Irrigation: Moist soil condition required

Fertilizer Recommendation (Per Acre)

FertilizerQuantity
Urea100 kg
SSP / DAP125 kg / 50 kg
MOP50 kg

Note: Apply fertilizers along both sides of rows.

Balbharani Method

  1. Light irrigation one day before operation
  2. Apply fertilizers uniformly in furrows
  3. Mix fertilizer with soil
  4. Perform earthing-up properly
  5. Ensure proper drainage

  • Avoid excess nitrogen
  • Ensure proper earthing-up
