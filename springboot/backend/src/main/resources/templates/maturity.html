✓ Do’s — Best Practices

Monitor Brix levels Regularly check sugar content using a refractometer. Uniform top and bottom Brix reading = mature cane.
Prevent flowering Avoid conditions that trigger tasselling. Flowering shifts energy away from sugar storage and reduces yield.
Remove weeds Keep the field clean. Weeds compete for soil moisture and interfere with harvesting equipment.
Clean the crop Remove lodged or broken canes. These are prone to rot, pest attacks, and lower sugar quality.
Coordinate with factories Contact your local sugar factory to align harvest timing with their crushing season schedule.

✗ Don’ts — What to Stop

Stop nitrogen application Late nitrogen promotes vegetative growth and tillering, which depletes stored sugar and delays maturity.
Stop all fertilizers Fertilizer application should stop well before harvest to let the plant focus on sucrose accumulation.
Stop irrigation Stop watering 15–20 days before harvest. This water stress forces the plant into ripening mode and concentrates sugar.

What is Brix: Brix is the measurement of total soluble solids (mainly sugar) in sugarcane juice. Higher Brix indicates higher sugar content and maturity.
