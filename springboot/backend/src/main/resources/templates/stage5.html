Stage V : Harvesting Stage of Sugarcane

Overview

The harvesting stage is the final phase in the growth cycle of sugarcane. It typically occurs 10 to 18 months after planting, depending on the variety and climatic conditions.


Signs of Maturity:
  • Leaves start drying and turning yellow
  • Internodes become well-developed and firm
  • Juice becomes sweet with high sugar content
  • Growth rate slows down
Harvesting Methods
  • Manual Harvesting: Cutting canes close to the ground using knives or machetes
  • Mechanical Harvesting: Use of machines for large-scale farming

Important Practices:
  • Harvest at the right maturity stage for maximum sugar yield
  • Avoid cutting too early or too late
  • Remove dry leaves and tops before transport
  • Transport quickly to mills to prevent sugar loss
Post-Harvest Handling
  • Clean and bundle canes properly
  • Minimize delay between harvesting and processing
  • Maintain moisture to reduce weight loss

Conclusion:

Proper harvesting techniques and timing are crucial to ensure high sugar recovery and better quality produce.

Best Practices for Sugarcane Harvesting

1. Timing: How to Know When to Harvest
Harvesting at peak maturity ensures the highest sucrose content.

Maturity Window: Early varieties usually mature in 10–11 months, while mid-season or late varieties may take 12–18 months.

Visual Checks: Leaves turn yellow and dry, and the stalk will harden.

Metallic Sound Test: Mature cane gives sharp metallic sound.

Brix Test: Use a hand refractometer to measure Brix (sugar) level. A reading of 18% to 20% indicates peak maturity.
2. Harvesting Technique
A. Manual harvesting: Common on smaller farms and needs skilled labour to avoid damaging future crops.

B. Mechanical harvesting: Mechanical harvesters are faster and more efficient for large, uniform fields.
3. Critical Post-Harvest Steps
Once cane is cut, sugar begins to degrade immediately.

24-Hour Rule: Cane should reach mill within 24 hours. Any delay beyond this causes a rapid drop in sugar quality and weight.

Prevent Moisture Loss: If transport is delayed, cover the harvested cane piles with trash (dry leaves) and sprinkle them with water to keep them cool.
4. Managing the Ratoon (The Next Crop)
Sugarcane is perennial, so harvest technique affects next year's growth.

Stubble Shaving: For manual harvest, shave the remaining "stubble" down to ground level to ensure uniform sprouting of the next crop.

Trash Mulching: Instead of burning the leftover leaves (trash), spread them across the field. This helps retain soil moisture, prevents weeds, and adds organic matter back into the soil.

How Farmers Should Contact Sugar Factories

1. Contact Through Village Sugar Society

Village society forwards this information to factory agriculture department

Farmers should inform village sugar society when crop is ready:

  • Farmer Name
  • Survey Number
  • Registered Area
  • Variety (Co-86032)
  • Crop Maturity Status
2. Contact Factory Agriculture Officer
    Farmer may directly contact:
  • Factory Agriculture Officer
  • Cane Development Officer
    • Farmer should provide:
  • Crop readiness information
  • Field location
  • Registered area
  • Expected harvesting date

  • Inform crop maturity
  • Confirm cutting date
  • Discuss labour availability
